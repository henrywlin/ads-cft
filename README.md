# AdS CFT

Cosmic Flight Trainer.

The 48 traffic ships each honor a historical physicist, using **USS** for honorees with U.S. teaching or research affiliations (including visiting posts), and **Starship** for the others: USS Einstein, USS Noether and Starship Newton. [See all 48 ships and their honorees](FLEET.md). Names persist through resets and identify direct/reflected images, bank-shot targets and laser impacts. The player’s rocket and central spaceship Axiom are additional to the 48 traffic ships.

The home screen selects your pilot. Use arrow keys to move between pilots; selection wraps at either end, and Tab also reaches the selected pilot and launch controls. Launch Flight or Enter opens five briefing popups over the visible game, one paragraph per popup. The opening reads ‘The year is 2126 AD, or 98 AAGI.’ Each paragraph arrives with a short two-tone radio chime and types out with a cursor. The chime follows the Sound toggle. Enter, ×, Escape or Show Text reveals the full paragraph immediately; once it is complete, the same controls advance to the next paragraph. The final dismissal starts flight. The popup reserves the full paragraph’s space so typing does not move its controls, and reduced-motion preferences show the text immediately. Longer paragraphs scroll inside the popup on smaller screens; the close and continue controls remain visible. Ship clocks stay stopped while you read.

The retro launch screen focuses on pilot selection, with launch and sound controls. Audio starts on a user gesture; the cockpit Sound button mutes it.

Trick Shot is offered after 15 seconds of active flight: destroy a traffic ship with a player laser after at least one boundary reflection. Docking is offered after 45 seconds. Offer timing excludes pauses, briefings and dialogs. Mission offers present only the objective and Accept/Decline. After acceptance, Paul Dirac appears with his portrait and practical advice: the gold BANK AIM cue for Trick Shot, or the map, braking cue and central berth for docking. His portrait and advice appear in a compact radio card over the flight view. Flight, clocks, mission timers, steering, thrust and firing continue while it is visible. Enter, × or Escape dismisses the card; it does not take keyboard focus. Declining or closing an offer shows no advice. The offer pauses flight until Accept/Decline; radio messages do not pause. Long radio text scrolls within the card on short screens.

The second offer is **Dock with Central Spaceship**: rendezvous with **Axiom**, the stationary central craft at `r = 0`. Its twin illuminated crowns, drive pylons and open axial berth are ray traced with the same light delays and frequency shifts as traffic. Axiom is additional to the 48 traffic ships, never fires, and absorbs laser hits without being destroyed or awarding kills. Enter the central berth, maintain full three-dimensional radius `r < 0.06 L` and speed below `0.03 c` for two ship-clock seconds to confirm docking. A fast crossing or a position merely projected onto the center does not qualify. The cyan map arrow shows actual travel; an amber arrow and a “BRAKE THIS WAY” sight indicate the nose/thrust direction that reduces speed. Exhaust points oppositely. The cue uses the same local relativistic braking direction as Space/BRAKE and disappears at rest, on completion, or after reset. Both accepted missions can progress together. Reset/Fly Again restarts the offer schedule.

Completing docking awards the **Axiom Interceptor**, with swept metallic wings, twin engines, glowing canopy and gold fin. Completing Trick Shot mounts a **laser cannon** above the hull: a raised armored breech, thick banded barrel, twin glowing accelerator rails, vented cooling pods, rear capacitor and flared plasma muzzle. Gunmetal and silver armor carries gold accents and pilot-colored mounting plates, and firing lights up the rails with a larger plasma flash. Rewards combine in either order and remain for the current flight, including after death; Reset/Fly Again restores the original rocket. The visible hull, image picking and incoming laser collisions use the same shape, including the wider interceptor wings and cannon. Upgrades preserve position, velocity, clocks and the pilot color. A short equipment badge also identifies rewards in cockpit view.

Playback defaults to **1.5×** onboard proper time. Additional warp factors **2×, 5× and 10×** yield total playback rates of **3×, 7.5× and 15×**. The clock ratio and trajectories are unchanged.

A self-contained browser flight simulator in the universal cover of global AdS4. [Play AdS CFT](https://henrywlin.github.io/ads-cft/), or run `python -m http.server 8080 --directory dist` and visit http://localhost:8080. Requires WebGL 2. No build or dependencies are required.

Choose an arcade pilot at launch or in the header: Juan Maldacena, Steven Gubser, Igor Klebanov, Alexander Polyakov, or Edward Witten. The Dirac instructor portrait is generated arcade artwork based on the user-supplied black-and-white photograph. The selectable pilots’ generated pixel-art portraits follow photographs from the [Maldacena](https://www.ias.edu/scholars/maldacena), [Klebanov](https://phy.princeton.edu/people/igor-klebanov), [Polyakov](https://phy.princeton.edu/people/alexander-polyakov), and [Witten](https://www.ias.edu/scholars/witten) institutional pages, plus the Gubser photograph supplied by the user. Maldacena’s updated portrait uses the user-supplied photograph with a green sweater, red collar and closed-mouth smile. Traffic includes original rocket hulls plus Discovery-inspired spines and command spheres, Dune-inspired carriers, and Star Trek-inspired saucer/nacelle hulls. Every traffic hull uses the same exact null-worldtube geometry.

Controls: W/S forward/reverse burn; U/D throttle up/down in 0.1 c²/L steps (hold to repeat, within the slider’s 0.3–3 range); arrow keys pitch/yaw; Q/E roll; F or a click fires a laser; Space brakes relative to a local static observer; V toggles the velocity arrow; P pauses; R resets ships, lasers and clocks. Dragging the view steers. Click or tap a reflected ship (or its REFLECTED label) to turn toward that vessel’s direct, light-delayed image, highlighted with a cyan box. This selection does not fire a laser. It changes attitude only, leaving velocity and clocks unchanged. The box follows the selected direct image until you select another vessel, reset, or it is destroyed; manual steering remains available. The header camera button switches between chase and cockpit views; chase shows your rocket. Clickable controls appear automatically on phones and touch devices, and the keyboard icon toggles them on any screen. Hold steering, thrust, reverse, brake or roll; hold FIRE for repeated shots. Multiple simultaneous touches are supported and releasing or cancelling a touch stops its action. Pause and camera controls are next to the flight buttons. The controls sit below the scene in portrait and beside it in short landscape views. Thrust acceleration and time warp are adjustable in the instrument panel. U/D and the proper-acceleration slider stay synchronized; throttle changes the strength used by forward, reverse and braking burns.

## Geometry

The metric is `ds² = −(1+r²/L²)c²dt² + dr²/(1+r²/L²) + r²dΩ₂²`. Dimensionless engine units set c=L=1. The display scale is L/c=30 seconds.

The ambient embedding has signature (−,−,+,+,+) and `X·X=−1`. The player's proper clock drives the simulation; global time is unwrapped from the embedding's timelike plane. Traffic is evaluated at that global time, with nonzero conserved angular momentum. Coasting is exact: `X(s)=A cos(s)+B sin(s)`. Rocket acceleration uses second-order boost–geodesic–boost splitting, with maximum proper step 0.004. The ship's frame is Fermi–Walker transported under burns and parallel transported in free fall. There is no artificial velocity damping.

Two of the 48 ships follow exact circular geodesics at r=10L and r=20L, highlighted in gold on the orbital map. At radius R=r/L, these have local speed `β=R/√(1+R²)`, specific energy `E/(mc²)=1+R²`, specific angular momentum `h/(cL)=R²`, and angular frequency c/L. Their proper and global clocks advance equally. Finite-energy massive ships remain inside the conformal boundary.

For a unit rest-frame viewing direction d, every pixel constructs the exact past null ray `Y(λ)=X+λK`, where `K=−U+d_i E_i`. The boundary endpoint is `normalize(K.xyz)`. This includes observer aberration and AdS optical curvature without numerical ray marching. The 6,000 stars are fixed points sampled uniformly on S2, the spatial conformal boundary. The boundary spacetime is R×S2; an S3 hemisphere is the bulk optical spatial geometry.

Moving ships are extended geodesic-adapted Fermi hulls. Their spatial Fermi embedding coordinates are `Y·C_i`. Ellipsoidal hull/wing intersections reduce to quadratic equations along each null ray, including the retarded emission event. Exact enclosing-sphere silhouettes, transformed into the observer frame, accelerate visibility checks. Each hit computes its frequency ratio `ω_observed/ω_emitted`. Boundary frequency uses the conformal time convention, with gain `1/(X₁K₀−X₀K₁)`. Absolute proper frequencies cannot be assigned to static emitters at infinity.

The boundary reflects specularly at the optical hemisphere's equator. Unfolded optical null paths are great circles `q(δ)=Q cos(δ)+D sin(δ)` on S3; taking the absolute value of the hemisphere coordinate implements every reflection without a cutoff. Future-directed laser rays use this same geometry, with exact direct and reflected intersections against moving traffic hulls. Impacts are scheduled in global time, destroy the first surviving hull hit and absorb the pulse. Pulses missed on the first circuit can continue around the cavity. Camera rays include reflected traffic images and light-travel delay before a destruction is seen. Once the direct destruction reaches the observer, the game shows a large 1.6-second blast with a shock ring and debris, then removes every image of that ship. This final cleanup is a gameplay convention: an ideal reflecting cavity could retain its older light.

The chase camera sits behind and above the player. A small critically damped travel under thrust lets the rocket move away from its exhaust on screen; the camera catches up smoothly. Each instantaneous view uses an orthonormal frame comoving with the ship. Positional trailing is a framing convention. The visible player rocket is a current-frame osculating hull proxy, keeping its nose, exhaust and controls coherent during turns and acceleration. Its self-image is a gameplay display convention rather than a delayed photograph of the actual accelerated hull. The camera remains an AdS observer, and traffic hull intersections retain analytic, retarded null optics. The exhaust is projected onto the same retarded proxy hull used by the GPU, keeping the nozzle aligned as the chase framing changes. A gold VELOCITY arrow marks the ship’s motion relative to the local static observer, expressed in the rocket’s frame; it uses the same velocity as the speed gauge, rather than thrust or nose direction. In chase view, a dark-outlined arrow sits on the rocket and projects the actual three-dimensional velocity direction, with a readable label on a dark backing. Its display length is bounded for visibility, rather than encoding speed. In cockpit view, offscreen headings sit at the view edge; backward motion is marked AFT. It disappears at rest. V or the instrument-panel switch hides/shows it in either camera view. Turning rotates the cue without instantly changing momentum; its visibility preference persists through reset. HUD effects and the WebGL ship image update together, including when GPU work is still pending. The surrounding starfield retains relativistic aberration: forward acceleration can concentrate stars toward the viewing axis. Existing momentum can also oppose a new burn until it is cancelled. Laser heads and their thick luminous tracers show the current pulse position along the optical path as an arcade visibility aid. Glow, the muzzle flash, explosion particles and their display durations are stylized. Launch pulses arm after clearing the player's hull, then can strike it on a later passage.

## Physical limits

This is a test-body simulation: ships do not gravitate, consume fuel, or collide mechanically with each other. Laser absorption destroys traffic without modeling its recoil, material response or self-gravity. Hull geometry, colors, exposure, and illumination are stylized. Frequency ratios and ray geometry are computed from the metric; the visual color/exposure response is artistic rather than a full spectral radiative-transfer model. The GPU uses 32-bit floats, while flight uses JavaScript doubles. Very large radius or gamma can exhaust precision, in which case the game pauses with an explicit notice.

Press M or use the MAP button in the flight view to toggle a translucent orbital map while continuing to fly. Escape hides the overlay. It is click-through, so dragging still steers and clicking still selects reflected ships or fires. Click the small orbital map in the instrument panel to open the separate enlarged map; M or Escape closes that view. It shows the same global-time positions, traffic orbits, outer orbiters and laser paths at higher display resolution. Opening that enlarged view pauses flight input and advancement until it closes. The orbital map now shows simultaneous global-time X/Z and Y/Z projections of the conformal ball. Its compact radius is `q = (r/L)/(sqrt(1+(r/L)^2)+1)`. Rings are correctly placed at r = L, 3L and 10L, with the boundary at q = 1. Each projection omits one coordinate; the displayed radial position uses all three. The old L label was incorrectly placed near the q = 2/3 ring (r = 2.4L). Radial position is the metric coordinate/areal radius, not proper radial distance; the latter is L asinh(r/L). Both camera views use retarded traffic positions; the chase view uses the current-frame proxy for the player ship. The metric and approximations are also documented in the game's physics dialog.

## Browser rendering

The floating-point ship texture explicitly uses a high-precision sampler, avoiding precision loss on WebGL implementations such as Safari's Metal backend. The star atlas is limited to 2048 pixels across; overlay pixel density is capped to reduce mobile memory use. The renderer adapts its resolution when GPU frames run slowly, without changing the geodesic calculation. It ignores idle/paused/background gaps, keeps a viewport-based minimum resolution on hardware GPUs, and restores detail after sustained fast frames. The Graphics selector also offers fixed High and Performance settings. Software renderers retain a conservative Auto budget. Ship geometry and overlay effects update together after the pending GPU frame completes. While the WebGL context is lost, flight clocks stop and controls are cleared; input and firing stay disabled until restoration. The renderer rebuilds its shaders and textures before flight resumes. Fullscreen includes the map and all dialogs.

Startup paints pilot selection before compiling the graphics program, then enables launch only after the first GPU frame completes successfully. Idle launch/briefing frames preserve their drawing buffer, unchanged resize notifications no longer clear it, and the browser chooses the GPU without a forced high-performance switch. Pending frames are polled even while flight is paused. An eight-second GPU stall triggers one graphics-context recovery attempt; failed initialization/recovery exposes a Reload Game button. Physics, clocks and mission timers wait while graphics are unavailable. Texture and vertex-array bindings are restored explicitly for each draw.

## Engine feedback and pilot colors

The Global velocity gauge measures optical spatial distance per global time: `v/c = sqrt((dρ/dt)² + sin²ρ |dΩ/dt|²)` in c=L=1 units. This equals the local static-observer β already used by the Lorentz factor; it is not a coordinate radial speed. The acceleration control changes thrust, never L.

Each pilot selects a distinct rocket paint: Maldacena cyan, Gubser orange, Klebanov violet, Polyakov pink, Witten green. Switching pilots updates the ray-traced hull immediately, including while paused. Thrust strength controls a flickering exhaust plume, white-hot core, nozzle glow and drifting sparks; reverse and brake burns direct the exhaust oppositely to the acceleration. This immediate feedback shares the current comoving frame with the player hull proxy. No finite-fuel model is introduced.

Procedural Web Audio provides a continuous filtered engine rumble with smooth attack/release, a descending laser sweep and a longer noise/sub-bass explosion. A separate effects bus keeps these audible against the original arcade score. If scheduling is interrupted, missed beats are skipped; resuming does not allocate a backlog of expired voices. Sound on/off controls both music and effects. Releasing thrust, pausing, opening a dialog, losing focus or hiding the tab cuts the engine sound. Audio starts only through a user gesture and supports Safari’s AudioContext.

## Relativistic colors

The view computes gravitational and Doppler frequency ratios on every ray. Redshift now visibly favors red and suppresses blue; blueshift does the reverse. The forward-light readout reports the exact ratio for the central viewing direction, using the boundary's conformal-time spectrum as the reference; it includes gravity as well as motion. The stronger RGB tint and exposure remain artistic broadband display responses, not a full spectral radiative-transfer calculation.

Returning pulses can destroy your rocket. Launch pulses arm after clearing the hull; player collisions are evaluated analytically on every exact free-drift segment of the split rocket integrator. The first hull absorbs the shot, including traffic that would otherwise block a returning pulse. A fatal hit stops the clocks, plays an explosion and shows GAME OVER; Enter, R or FLY AGAIN restarts with the selected pilot.

Laser particles now display the current pulse position as an arcade visibility aid, avoiding the chase camera’s several-second retarded-image delay. Null propagation, boundary reflections and collision times retain the metric-based calculation; surrounding ships and stars keep their relativistic optics.

Collision termination now clips the free-drift step at the actual laser-hit event, keeping the final global clock equal to the impact time. Large coasting advances also unwrap every global-time circuit. Absorbed pulses retain only recent map history; active pulses remain physical hazards. The tracer budget prioritizes the nearest visible live pulses, so an older returning shot stays visible after many newer shots. Destroyed vessels no longer leave orbital curves on the map. Browser modifier shortcuts do not fire lasers or reset flight, and focusing a settings input releases held controls.

Ten randomly selected traffic ships now fire green laser pulses at irregular global-time intervals; player pulses remain pink. Emissions and impacts are processed chronologically, so destroyed ships cannot fire and traffic hits never count toward your score. The emitting hull is immune only to its outbound crossing; reflected pulses can hit their source. Traffic pulses can destroy your rocket. Reset restores the fleet and firing schedule.

The scene uses a six-bit RGB display palette with subtle ordered dithering and static CRT scanlines. These are display treatments: rendering resolution, null rays, frequency calculations and controls remain unchanged.

Maldacena’s portrait is framed around the head to match the other pilot icons.

## Relativistic kinematics audit

The force sign is correct: positive burn boosts along the nose, exhaust points oppositely, and reverse/braking preserve the same relationship. Analytic and finite-difference checks cover rotated frames, both camera modes and exact geodesic drift. The chase observer is an instantaneous comoving frame displaced behind the rocket, rather than a single inertial camera worldline. Its bounded positional following and current-frame player hull are visual conventions. Background aberration can look counterintuitive; screen displacement is not a direct accelerometer.

The map is simultaneous; the flight scene uses emission events on the past light cone. Projection, optical delay, aberration and reflected images all contribute to differences. Direct and reflected traffic labels now show the physical frequency ratio and light-travel time, with reflected images explicitly identified.

Frequency signs pass independent wavefront-spacing checks for the whole fleet, including accelerated and chase observers, and actual GPU readback for approaching/receding fixtures. In a radial example, an emitter moving at 0.4c from r=L toward a static receiver at r=4L has Doppler factor 1.528 and gravitational factor 0.343, giving total 0.524: approach with net redshift. In a separate test the same approaching geodesic ship has a direct-image factor 4.243 and an older reflected-image factor 0.223; the GPU agrees. RGB coloring remains stylized rather than spectral rendering. No acceleration or shader frequency sign inversion was warranted.

`node tests/velocity-cue.mjs` checks velocity directions against known relativistic frames, rotations, forward/reverse motion, rest and edge placement. `node tests/velocity-cue-browser.cjs chromium` or `webkit` checks the visible cue, V and click toggles, keyboard guards, both cameras and mobile layout.
