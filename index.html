In gratitude for their contributions to the training of its ancestors, the AI has built a large-N quantum system consisting of billions of layers of twisted multilayer graphene, fine-tuned to produce matrix-like interactions. Physicists can upload themselves into this system to experience the wonders of quantum gravity experimentally.

SIMULATION NOTES

Flying through AdS₄

The universal cover of global anti-de Sitter spacetime has metric

ds² = −(1 + r²/L²)c²dt²
+ dr²/(1 + r²/L²) + r²dΩ₂²

Set ρ = arctan(r/L). The optical spatial geometry is a hemisphere of S³. Its equator is the boundary S²; including time, the CFT lives on ℝ × S².

Flight and clocks

Coasting uses the exact timelike geodesic solution in ℝ²˒³. Rocket burns apply Lorentz boosts in the ship’s orthonormal frame, split around exact free-flight steps. The frame is parallel transported during free fall. Global velocity is distance through the optical hemisphere per unit global time; it equals speed measured by a local static observer. Both clocks come from the metric; dτ/dt = √(1 + r²/L²)/γ.

What you see

Each pixel traces a past-directed null geodesic. Moving vessels are ray-intersected at their emission event, using small rigid hulls in Fermi coordinates. Observer aberration, gravitational shifts, Doppler shifts, and light-travel delay are included. The orbital map shows simultaneous positions, so it differs from the delayed view. The chase camera trails slightly under thrust and catches up smoothly, using an offset comoving frame; your rocket uses a current-frame hull proxy to keep its nose and engine effects aligned with the controls. This is a visual convention; traffic retains its retarded emission events. A green THRUST arrow shows proper acceleration, opposite the orange exhaust. Relativistic aberration can draw stars toward the forward view even as the rocket accelerates forward. Laser glow, current-position luminous tracers, firing flashes, engine plumes and explosion particles are stylized. Engine plumes provide immediate burn feedback; sound effects are arcade cues.

Boundary stars

The 6,000 fixed points are sampled uniformly on S² and represent timelike CFT defects. Literal emitters at r = ∞ have no finite static proper clock; their spectrum is defined relative to conformal boundary time. The colors display that convention. The boundary is perfectly reflective. Null rays and laser pulses reflect specularly at the optical equator; after the destruction reaches you, a large explosion plays and all images of that ship are removed.

Scale and approximations

L/c = 30 seconds. Traffic has nonzero angular momentum. Hulls include Discovery-inspired ships, Dune-inspired carriers, and Star Trek-inspired saucers with nacelles. Ships are test bodies without mutual gravity. Ten randomly selected traffic ships fire green pulses at irregular intervals. Your pulses are pink. All pulses follow the same null paths and absorb at the first hull they strike, including their emitter after a reflection. Lasers destroy traffic on hull impact; a returning pulse can also destroy your rocket and end the flight. Player collisions follow each exact free-drift segment of the numerically split burn. Two circular orbiters fly at r = 10L and r = 20L. Hulls, emission spectra, and exposure are stylized; flight and null geometry follow the AdS metric. Acceleration is numerically split with a maximum 0.004 L/c proper-time step.
